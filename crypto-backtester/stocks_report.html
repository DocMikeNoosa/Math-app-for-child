Quality Stock Backtest

Nine stocks picked in October 2026 for low debt, high profits, heavy R&D and moderate volatility, all up more than the S&P 500 over the past year. They are tested with the same strategies as the crypto backtest, plus portfolio strategies. A rule-based version picks stocks each month using only what was known at the time. Trades assume Interactive Brokers: 0.01% commission plus 0.05% slippage.

The short answer. Holding the nine picks since 2016 turned $10k into $377,079 (+40% a year) against $47,696 for the S&P 500. That number is mostly hindsight: these stocks were chosen today because they did well.

  • The fair test (a rule that picked hot, steady stocks each month from that month's S&P 500) made +15% a year since 2012, the same as the S&P 500's +15%, with a bigger worst drop (-51% vs -34%). Simple momentum did a bit better (+18%) but still trailed the Nasdaq-100 (+20%).
  • Timing individual stocks with trend or dip-buying rules lost to simply holding them in nearly every case. The best rule beat holding on only 2 of 9 stocks. Trend rules did cut the worst drops.
  • Among the picks, rotating into the 4 strongest each month earned the most (+46% a year). That result is just as inflated by hindsight.

The fair test rule-based, 2012-01-03 to 2026-10-07

Each month, take the companies that were in the S&P 500 that month (including ones later removed). Keep those with one-year volatility of 55% or less that are above their 200-day average. Buy the 10 with the best 12-month return, skipping the most recent month. This copies the "hot and steady" part of the screen without hindsight. It can't copy the profit and debt checks, because past fundamentals aren't in the data.

How to read this

  1. Hindsight bias is the biggest trap in stock backtests. A list of today's best companies always looks brilliant in the past. The rule-based test is the honest estimate.
  2. Concentration risk. Nine stocks, seven of them in semiconductors, networking and software, can fall together. Their past worst drops ran from -41% to -67%.
  3. Data. Daily prices from the public Johnbrick123/sp500-data dataset (split- and dividend-adjusted, point-in-time S&P 500 membership), spot-checked against known prices and stock splits. Company profit and debt figures come from fiscal 2025 annual reports (Nvidia's fiscal 2026); a few R&D figures couldn't be verified and are marked.
  4. Not included: taxes, and interest on idle cash.
