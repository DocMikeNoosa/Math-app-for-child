Crypto Strategy Backtest

Twelve strategies run on real daily Bitcoin and Ethereum prices: trend-following, mean reversion, sentiment, and combinations of them. Each trade pays a 0.40% Kraken Pro taker fee plus 0.10% slippage. Signals use only data available at that day's close and are traded at the next day's open.

The short answer (written for the bundled run below). Combining several trend signals and cutting position size when volatility is high (Ensemble + vol targeting) was the most consistent strategy. It had the best risk-adjusted return on both coins and roughly halved the worst crash. It did not reliably make more money than simply holding.

  • Bitcoin: $75,927 vs $58,716 for buy & hold, worst drop -44% vs -82%.
  • Ethereum: $98,975 vs $191,915 for buy & hold, worst drop -41% vs -79%.
  • Mean reversion (RSI, Bollinger) and the contrarian sentiment rule lost money or badly lagged. Adding the sentiment layer to trend-following made results worse, not better.
  • Picking each year's strategy using only past results earned less than buy & hold on both coins.

Parameter sensitivity

The simple "price above its N-day average" rule gives very different results depending on N:

50-day: +35%/yr · 100-day: +29%/yr · 150-day: +32%/yr · 200-day: +17%/yr · 250-day: +18%/yr

Small changes in a setting swing results by double digits. That's a sign the edge is noisy, and that a setting tuned to the past may not hold up.

Parameter sensitivity

The simple "price above its N-day average" rule gives very different results depending on N:

50-day: +46%/yr · 100-day: +30%/yr · 150-day: +38%/yr · 200-day: +43%/yr · 250-day: +31%/yr

Small changes in a setting swing results by double digits. That's a sign the edge is noisy, and that a setting tuned to the past may not hold up.

How to read this

  1. Sentiment data. This run used a price-based market-mood score (momentum, volatility and volume). The real Fear & Greed Index and historical news headlines couldn't be downloaded from the environment this was built in. Running python run.py --fear-greed on your own computer uses the real index.
  2. Choosing the best of twelve is biased. Some strategy will always look best on past data by luck. The walk-forward test is the fairer estimate, and it lagged buy & hold.
  3. Past results don't predict future ones. Most of the gains came from a few huge bull runs (2020–21, 2023–24). In a long sideways market, trend strategies lose small amounts repeatedly.
  4. Not included: taxes (every sell can be a taxable event), interest on idle cash, exchange outages, and execution delays beyond the slippage allowance.
  5. "Worst drop" is the largest fall from a previous high. Sharpe is return per unit of volatility; higher is better, and buy & hold is the reference.
